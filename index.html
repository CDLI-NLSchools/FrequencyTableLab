Step 2 — Build the frequency table

Count the values the way you would on paper, one interval at a time. Click a row in the table to choose your interval, then click each value above that belongs in it: the value strikes through and a tally appears in the row. Click a struck value again to undo it. When every value is struck through, every value has been counted exactly once.

Intervals are written 0-5, 5-10, and so on. A value that lands exactly on a boundary counts in the interval it opens: exactly 15 minutes belongs in 15-20, not in 10-15. That way no value is ever counted twice.

Step 3 — Generate the histogram from YOUR table

The bars are drawn from the numbers in your table — if a bar looks wrong, the mistake is in the counting, not the drawing.


Step 4 — Answer from your graph

What a quality answer includes

  • For a): name the tallest interval (or both intervals, if two tie), give its frequency, and say in plain words what that means about the students — not just which bar is tallest.
  • For b): show the actual addition of the interval frequencies, and say why those intervals are the ones that count.

a) Which interval is tallest in your histogram, and what does that tell you about the students?

b) How many students spent 15 or more minutes on their math homework? Show how you got the number from your table.

Worked example — a different class

Another class of 25 students recorded these math homework times: 23, 2, 22, 7, 10, 10, 23, 9, 13, 29, 13, 12, 14, 18, 25, 12, 19, 19, 20, 12, 12, 23, 5, 13, 7.

Their frequency table: 0–5: 1 student; 5–10: 4; 10–15: 10; 15–20: 3; 20–25: 5; 25–30: 2.

a) The tallest interval is 10–15 minutes, with 10 of the 25 students. That tells me most students spent a moderate amount of time on their math homework, roughly 10 to 15 minutes, and almost nobody spent next to none: only 1 student was under 5 minutes.

b) “15 or more” means the 15–20, 20–25 and 25–30 intervals. Adding their frequencies: 3 + 5 + 2 = 10 students.

Now compare with your answers above. If yours are missing something the example includes, edit them before you hand in your work.

Step 5 — Extend your thinking

c) The middle value of 25 values is the 13th one. Using only your frequency table (not the original list), work out which interval the median math homework time falls in. Explain how the table tells you.

d) The principal says: “Our students don't spend enough time on their math homework.” Using your histogram, argue for or against the principal. Refer to specific intervals and frequencies in your answer.
